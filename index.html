How to use this board
  1. Pick Men or Women at the top. Every tab switches with it.
  2. Start with Find my fits: choose a class and position (add an SAT or ACT score if you like) to get a ranked list of programs that are losing players at that position, with an academic read on each.
  3. Click any underlined school name for its profile: admissions numbers, testing policy, aid, NCAA eligibility, returning players by position, commits, feeder clubs and links to the roster, coaching staff and recruiting questionnaire.
  4. The openings tabs show every program's spots by position for 2027 through 2030. Use the Needs filter to see only schools losing players at one position, and click a row for names.
  5. The player pool tabs list recruits by class. Click a player for honors and best-fit schools; tap ☆ to build a shortlist, and use My school list on the All schools tab the same way. Both stay in this browser and download from the CSV buttons above.
  6. Feeders & trends shows which clubs and high schools send players where, and which programs are behind last year's pace.
  7. Contacting coaches has guidelines for emailing coaches and a verified directory of every program's coaching staff with email and phone.

How to reach college coaches, and every program's head coach with contact details. Division I coaches can reply from June 15 after sophomore year, but you can write to them before that; they read it and keep notes.

Guidelines

  • Write it yourself. Email from the player's own address, not a parent's, and address the head coach plus the assistant who recruits your area. One school per email, never a group email.
  • Lead with the facts coaches sort by: grad year, position, dominant hand, club and high school, and cap number and color for upcoming events. Put them in the subject line and the first two lines.
  • Say why that school. One specific sentence about the program or academics shows you have done your homework.
  • Keep it short. It should fit on one phone screen. Add height and wingspan, GPA and test scores (or test-optional), honors, your NCAA Eligibility Center ID and your club and high school coaches' contacts.
  • Always send video: a two-to-three-minute highlight reel with your best clips first and yourself marked before each clip, plus one full unedited game with your cap number and color noted. Coaches trust full games more than highlights.
  • Show what your position needs: goals and assists for attackers; drawn ejections and holding position for centers; steals and help defense for defenders; saves, outlets and communication for goalies.
  • Fill out the recruiting questionnaire (linked on each school profile) the same week you email.
  • Follow up around events. Email before Junior Olympics, ODP and big high school tournaments with your schedule and cap, and after with results and new video. Reply to any coach within a day.
  • Keep a log of every school, contact date and reply. The ☆ school list and the CSV download on this board are a good start.

Before June 15 after sophomore year, a Division I coach can send camp information and questionnaires but can't talk recruiting with the player. That is why verbal commitments come in a wave starting in the fall of junior year, and why women's classes, which recruit earlier, fill faster.

Visits

  • Official visits (paid by the school) have been unlimited in number since 2023, but only one per school and no longer than 48 hours. The school can cover travel and meals for the recruit and up to two family members.
  • Unofficial visits (paid by the family) are allowed any time, but meeting with coaches on one follows the Aug 1 before junior year date.

Commitments and signing

  • A verbal commitment is not binding on either side.
  • The National Letter of Intent was eliminated in late 2024. Recruits now sign an athletics aid agreement with the school. Written aid offers can't be made before Aug 1 of senior year, and once a recruit signs, other schools must stop recruiting them.
  • Ivy League schools (Harvard, Princeton, Brown) and Division III schools don't offer athletic scholarships, so a commitment there runs through admissions and need-based aid.

Roster limits and scholarships (House settlement)

Since July 1, 2025, Division I schools that opted into the House settlement no longer have a scholarship cap in water polo (it was 4.5 for men and 8 for women). Instead they have a roster limit of 24 players for both men and women, and they can give aid to any or all of those 24. Players who were already on a roster or already recruited when the rules changed are grandfathered and don't count against the limit.
  • What it means for recruits: schools carrying more than 24 now (shown when you expand a school) will likely sign fewer players than they lose until they're down to 24. Walk-on spots are scarcer than before.
  • Schools that didn't opt in, and Division II and III schools, aren't bound by the 24-player limit.

Transfer portal

  • Division I water polo has one 30-day transfer window a year, starting the day after the NCAA championship field is set. For men that has been early December to early January. For women it has been early May to early June.
  • Transfers can change a school's openings after this board was built, especially at the top programs.

Division II and III

  • Division II: phone calls, off-campus contact and official visits open June 15 after sophomore year. Unofficial visits are allowed any time.
  • Division III: no athletic scholarships. Coaches can call and message any time. Off-campus contact opens after sophomore year and official visits open January 1 of junior year.

Checklist for a recruit

  • Register with the NCAA Eligibility Center (needed for Division I and II) and keep the 16 core courses on track.
  • Fill out each target school's recruiting questionnaire, and send a short highlight video plus a full game along with your club and high school schedules.
  • Coaches watch the Junior Olympics, ODP regionals and nationals, and the top high school tournaments. Make sure your club and high school coaches know your target list.
  • Use the openings tabs to match your position to schools that are actually losing players at it. The Needs filter shows only the schools with an opening at one position.

How to read this board

  • These are the most likely openings, not promised spots. Seniors can take a fifth year, players transfer, and coaches fill some spots with transfers and international players.
  • Schools label positions differently. One program's utility player is another's attacker, so read attack and utility together.
  • Use the Needs filter to see only schools with an opening at one position, ranked by how many. Because schools label positions differently, check attack and utility together.
  • Commit positions come from forum posts; commits whose position wasn't posted reduce a school's total but not a specific position.
  • Women's openings use each school's newest posted roster. Schools that haven't posted their 2026-27 roster yet don't list this fall's freshmen, so their goalie counts can be low.
  • Division I schools that opted into the House settlement are capped at 24 rostered players, with current players grandfathered. UCLA, Air Force, Santa Clara, Navy, Pepperdine, UC Irvine, Brown, Pacific, LMU, UC San Diego and Stanford carry more than 24 now, so their real openings may be smaller than the departures shown. Expand a school to see its roster size.
  • The 24-player Division I limit applies to women too. UCLA, UC Davis, Stanford, La Salle, UC Irvine, Siena, Saint Mary's, Pacific and Iona carry more than 24 now, so their real openings may be smaller than the departures shown.
  • Conferences come from each conference's 2026 preseason poll (MPSF, Big West, WCC, WWPA, GCC, MAAC and the CWPA's NWPC and Mid-Atlantic conferences) plus SCIAC. Several programs change conferences in 2027-28; hover the ⓘ next to the conference filter for details.
  • Academic All-American honors come from USA Water Polo's 2023-24 and 2024-25 lists (a 3.5 GPA or higher). Those lists publish names only, with no club or school, so a player with a very common name could be matched to someone else. The 2025-26 list hasn't been published yet.
  • Division III programs offer no athletic scholarships and carry larger rosters, so their openings are softer than Division I numbers.
